# THE BAR — Nik's own Invity letter, written by hand

Written by Nik for the Invity Product Manager ad (`ad-invity-pm.txt` in this
directory), by hand, in about three hours. It is not a sample of the app's
output and nothing generated it. It is the TARGET: the pipeline is working when
its letter for this ad stands next to this one without embarrassment.

Read it before changing `prompts/letter-plan.js` or `prompts/cover-letter.js`.
It is the only artefact in this repo that shows what "good" means for a letter,
and it went unused from the day it was written until 2026-08-23.

## What it does that the pipeline did not

1. **Opens on a judgement about the employer**, and why that matters to him —
   not a thesis about the industry, not an announcement that he is applying.
2. **Takes a side.** "reflects people's needs and wants, not lectures about why
   they should want the tech" argues AGAINST something, which is how the ad's
   own fear gets answered.
3. **Two pieces of evidence, and the lesson they share** — Dezentrum,
   Blockchain4Humanity, "the core issue was always the same". Not a bundle of
   client names.
4. **Speaks in the first person, plainly.** "To me, users are the core."
5. **NO NUMBERS AT ALL.** The prompts push "the number, the change, the outcome"
   everywhere; the best letter in this project has none. A number is one kind of
   persuading detail, never the target.
6. Roughly 190 words.

---

Invity caught my eye because you are actually fixing Bitcoin's usability problem rather than just talking about it. Turning crypto into a simple, automated habit for everyday people is the exact product challenge I care about.

The +invity app reflects people's needs and wants, not lectures about why they should want the tech. I've pushed this message at Dezentrum in Zurich and worked to build it in projects like Blockchain4Humanity. The core issue was always the same: complex tech fails unless the user experience feels effortless. +invity's approach—combining trust, regulation, and strong execution—hits that exact sweet spot.

On the ground, I am a hands-on product manager who stays close to the details. To me, users are the core—it's their needs we need to build into specs, working closely with engineering and compliance to navigate trade-offs. Because I build custom AI systems and run fast discovery loops in my daily work today, fitting into an agile, AI-first product team is second nature.
